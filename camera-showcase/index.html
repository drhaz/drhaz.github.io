Camera showcase targets: u to Y across both hemispheres

← Daniel Harbeck's page

98 fields for the LCO 1-m network and a 26′ × 26′ camera: galaxies that fit the field, star-forming galaxies for the u band, and Galactic dust clouds for the optical-versus-Y comparison. Each preview is 40′ wide with the cyan box showing the camera footprint (north up, east left). Pick months and a hemisphere to see what is up; click any panel to enlarge it and step through u, g, r, i, z, Y. Download the full list as CSV.

u bandSDSS u in the north, SkyMapper u in the south. Where neither exists the panel shows GALEX NUV (0.23 µm), flagged in red.
g, r, i, zPan-STARRS DR1 down to δ ≈ −30°, then DESI Legacy Surveys DR10, DES DR2 or SkyMapper.
Y bandVISTA VVV Y in the southern Galactic plane and bulge, otherwise Pan-STARRS y or DES Y. Elsewhere in the far south it falls back to 2MASS J, flagged in red.
VisibilityHours per night with the Sun below −18° and the field above 30° (airmass < 2), on the 15th of each month. Best of McDonald and Teide (north) or CTIO, SAAO and Siding Spring (south).

This page used the hips2fits cutout service and the Sesame name resolver developed at CDS, Strasbourg Astronomical Observatory, France, the SIMBAD database (Wenger et al. 2000, A&AS 143, 9), the HiPS standard (Fernique et al. 2015, A&A 578, A114) and Astropy (Astropy Collaboration et al. 2022, ApJ 935, 167). Cutouts were reprojected, stretched and annotated for target selection only; use the original survey archives for any scientific measurement.

A field counts as visible in a month when it gets at least 2 h of dark time above 30° at one of the selected hemisphere's sites. Sites: McDonald (+30.7°) and Teide (+28.3°) in the north; CTIO (−30.2°), SAAO Sutherland (−32.4°) and Siding Spring (−31.3°) in the south. Panels are independent asinh stretches, so compare structure between bands, not absolute brightness. The surveys differ in depth and seeing, so the previews are a lower bound on what the new camera will show. Cutouts come from CDS hips2fits. Coordinates come from Sesame/SIMBAD, or are hand-picked field centres where a field frames several objects.
